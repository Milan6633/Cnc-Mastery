Multi-Axis CNC Machining (4 & 5 Axis)

4th-axis rotary setup, 3+2 positional machining, simultaneous 5-axis concepts, CAM strategies, and multi-axis workholding.

Lessons

01

Why Multi-Axis: From 3-Axis to 5-Axis Machining

Understand what a fixed single tool approach direction actually limits on a 3-axis mill, and how added rotary axes solve both setup count and surface finish problems at once.

45 minutesintermediate
→02

The 4th Axis: A-Axis and B-Axis Rotary Configurations

Add one rotary axis to a standard 3-axis mill and use it either for indexed positioning or continuous, synchronized rotation.

40 minutesintermediate
→03

3+2 Positional Machining: Tilted Work Planes

Lock two rotary axes to a fixed tilt, then cut with straightforward 3-axis motion in that tilted plane — capturing multi-axis access without full simultaneous-motion complexity.

40 minutesintermediate
→04

Simultaneous 5-Axis: Tool Vector Control and Lead/Lag/Tilt Angles

Move all five axes together continuously, actively steering the tool's vector with lead, lag, and tilt angles to optimize actual cutting conditions across a complex surface.

45 minutesadvanced
→05

Machine Kinematics: Trunnion Table vs. Swivel Head

Compare where a 5-axis machine's rotary motion actually lives — in the table or the spindle head — and the rigidity-versus-part-size tradeoff each configuration makes.

40 minutesadvanced
→06

Multi-Axis Toolpaths: Swarf Milling

Cut a tall, ruled wall in one efficient pass using the tool's side (flank) instead of its tip, replacing dozens of stepped passes with continuous flank contact.

40 minutesadvanced
→07

Multi-Axis Toolpaths: Flow-Line and Contour Strategies

Follow a surface's own natural curvature direction with flow-line finishing, or trace a genuinely 3D boundary with multi-axis contour, orienting the tool throughout with lead/lag/tilt.

40 minutesadvanced
→08

Collision Avoidance: Tool, Holder, Spindle, and Fixture Clearance

Extend collision checking from a fixed-orientation 3-axis toolpath to a continuously reorienting 5-axis one, where the moving fixture and machine structure itself become part of the risk.

45 minutesadvanced
→09

Axis Limits and Singularity Management

Respect a rotary axis's finite range of travel, and understand why a small change in tool orientation can suddenly demand an extreme, rapid axis movement near a kinematic singularity.

45 minutesadvanced
→10

Tool Reach Analysis and Tool-Length Considerations

Confirm a tool can physically reach every point on a multi-axis toolpath, then balance the reach a longer tool provides against the rigidity a shorter one keeps.

40 minutesadvanced
→11

Motion Optimization for Surface Quality and Cycle Time

Minimize unnecessary rotary motion and smooth tool orientation changes, balancing cycle time and finish quality against the lead/lag/tilt goals covered earlier in this module.

45 minutesadvanced
→12

5-Axis Vises and Dovetail Fixtures

Keep multi-axis workholding low-profile to avoid swinging into the spindle at extreme angles, and use a dovetail interface for fast, repeatable, self-locating fixture changes.

40 minutesintermediate
→13

Tombstone / Pallet-Mounted Fixturing

Present many parts or faces to a multi-axis spindle from one tombstone, and load the next job on a swappable pallet while the machine keeps running the current one.

40 minutesintermediate
→14

Zero-Point Clamping Systems

Mount a fixture or pallet to a precision, repeatable reference point in seconds, without re-indicating — the mechanism that actually makes fast multi-axis changeover practical.

40 minutesintermediate
→15

Choosing Between 3+2 and Simultaneous 5-Axis Machining

Decide feature by feature whether a job needs simultaneous 5-axis motion or can be handled with simpler 3+2 positioning, and see the two strategies combined on one real part.

50 minutesadvanced
