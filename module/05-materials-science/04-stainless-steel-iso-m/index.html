Stainless Steel (ISO M)

Classify stainless steels in the ISO M group and describe their machining characteristics.

Learning objectives

  • State the chromium content that defines stainless steel.
  • Explain the role of the Cr2O3 surface layer.
  • List the main stainless steel families.
  • Describe chip control and cutting forces when machining ISO M.

What Is Stainless Steel?

Stainless steel belongs to ISO group M and is a step up in difficulty from carbon steel.

  • Stainless steels are alloyed with a minimum of 11–12% chromium.
  • The carbon content is often low, down to a maximum of 0.01%.
  • The main alloying elements are Ni (nickel), Mo (molybdenum), and Ti (titanium).
Key concept

Chromium in the alloy reacts with the surrounding air to build a thin Cr2O3 skin on the surface. That passive layer is what blocks corrosion — and what earned the material its name.

Stainless Steel Families

ISO M is split into families that machine very differently from one another:

FamilyNotes
Ferritic / MartensiticEasier chip control of the group
AusteniticMost common; difficult chip control
Super-austeniticNi content ≥20%
Duplex (austenitic/ferritic)Mixed structure; difficult chip control

Machining Characteristics

Stainless steel is more demanding on both geometry and grade than ISO P steel.

  • It is a long-chipping material.
  • Chip control is fair in ferritic grades, and difficult in austenitic and duplex grades.
  • Chips off ISO M come out lamellar and uneven, and getting them there takes noticeably more force than a comparable cut in plain steel.
Safety warning

Expect a rougher ride when cutting stainless: forces climb, built-up edge tends to form, and the material work-hardens on top of the heat it generates. Together, that combination chews through cutting edges faster and makes chip control harder to hold onto.

Key concept

ISO M stainless carries a kc1 of 1800–2850 N/mm².

How well the chip breaks is really a function of the specific alloy and its heat treatment — some grades break cleanly, others fight you the whole way.

Self-Check

1. What minimum chromium content makes a steel “stainless”?

Show Answer

A minimum of 11–12% chromium.

2. What makes the stainless surface non-corrosive?

Show Answer

A Cr2O3 layer forms on the surface and makes the material non-corrosive.

3. Which stainless families give the most difficult chip control?

Show Answer

Austenitic and duplex stainless steels. Ferritic grades are easier to control.

4. What is the kc1 range for ISO M?

Show Answer

1800–2850 N/mm².
