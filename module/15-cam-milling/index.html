CAM Programming — 2.5D & 3-Axis

CAM workflow from model to G-code — Fusion 360 and Mastercam toolpaths, simulation, verification, and post-processors.

Lessons

01

CAD-to-CAM Workflow: Importing Geometry and Defining Stock

Bring a 3D model into CAM software and define the raw stock it's machined from — the two setup steps every CAM program starts with.

45 minutesintermediate
→02

Work Coordinate System Setup in CAM

Place a CAM setup's origin on a real, physically locatable feature that will match the machine's actual work offset at setup time.

40 minutesintermediate
→03

Tool Library Creation and Management

Keep a CAM tool library's geometry accurate to the real, physically assembled tools — the foundation every toolpath and collision check depends on.

40 minutesintermediate
→04

Defining Operations: Roughing, Finishing, and Drilling

Build a CAM program as a sequence of operations, each pairing a tool and geometry to a specific job, following the same rough-then-finish logic covered throughout the curriculum.

40 minutesintermediate
→05

Face Milling and 2D Contour/Profile Toolpaths

Generate a CAM toolpath to true a flat top surface, then cut a part's outer silhouette or an internal wall with a 2D contour toolpath.

40 minutesintermediate
→06

2D Pocket Toolpaths

Automate the rough-then-finish pocket strategy covered in manual machining into a CAM pocket toolpath, with the same tool-radius corner constraint carried over.

40 minutesintermediate
→07

Adaptive Clearing: High-Efficiency Roughing

Hold a constant, controlled radial tool engagement throughout an entire roughing operation, trading toolpath simplicity for dramatically higher metal removal rates and tool life.

45 minutesadvanced
→08

Slot Milling, Chamfer Milling, and Thread Milling

Three dedicated CAM toolpath types — slot, chamfer, and thread milling — each automating an operation covered elsewhere as a manual or single-purpose technique.

40 minutesintermediate
→09

Drilling Cycles in CAM: Spot, Peck, Bore, Ream, Tap

Generate the correct canned cycle automatically for each hole-making operation, and use rigid tapping's electronic spindle-to-feed synchronization for threaded holes.

40 minutesintermediate
→10

3D Roughing: Morphed Spiral and Pocket Clearing

Clear bulk material from a genuinely 3D surface model with a continuously flowing morphed spiral, or with simpler stacked Z-level slices.

45 minutesadvanced
→11

3D Finishing: Parallel and Scallop Strategies

Finish a 3D surface with simple parallel passes, or hold a truly constant scallop height everywhere with a scallop (constant-stepover) strategy.

45 minutesadvanced
→12

3D Finishing: Pencil and Steep-and-Shallow Strategies

Clean up internal corners a general finishing pass can't fully reach with pencil milling, and combine two strategies matched to local surface steepness.

45 minutesadvanced
→13

Rest Machining: Re-Machining with Smaller Tools

Send a smaller tool only where a larger roughing tool physically couldn't reach, rather than re-running an entire operation at the smaller tool's slower pace.

40 minutesadvanced
→14

Stepover and Cusp-Height Calculations

Work backward from a required surface finish to the maximum stepover a finishing tool can use, instead of guessing at a stepover value.

45 minutesadvanced
→15

Toolpath Simulation and Collision Detection

Verify a toolpath in software before it ever reaches the machine, checking not just the cutting tool but the holder and machine structure against the part and fixture.

40 minutesintermediate
→16

Stock Simulation: Material Removal Verification

Simulate material removal operation by operation to compare the resulting stock against the target model, catching leftover material or accidental over-cuts before they reach the machine.

40 minutesintermediate
→17

Post-Processing: Selecting and Configuring Post-Processors

Translate a CAM program's generic toolpath data into the specific G-code dialect a real machine and control actually understand, using the correct post-processor.

40 minutesintermediate
→18

NC Code Review and Editing Before Machine Upload

Read the actual, final G-code a post-processor produced before it ever reaches the machine — the last verification step before real material and a real spindle are involved.

40 minutesintermediate
→19

Setup Sheets: Documentation for the Shop Floor

Generate the setup sheet that tells whoever runs the job — who may not be who programmed it — exactly what tools, offsets, and workholding the program actually expects.

35 minutesbeginner
→20

Choosing the Right Toolpath Strategy

Pull together roughing, finishing, and verification strategy choices into one coherent plan for a real part, matched to its actual geometry and requirements.

50 minutesadvanced
