CAD for Manufacturing

3D parametric modeling with Fusion 360 and SolidWorks — sketching, extrude, revolve, design for manufacturability (DFM).

Lessons

01

Parametric Modeling: Sketches, Features, and the Design History Tree

Understand what makes parametric CAD different from simple 3D drawing — a recorded, editable history of features built on sketches.

50 minutesbeginner
→02

Sketch Constraints and Dimensions

Fully constrain a sketch with geometric relationships and dimensions so it behaves predictably as the model changes.

45 minutesbeginner
→03

Extrude: Bosses, Cuts, and Direction Options

Turn a 2D sketch into 3D geometry with extrude — adding material as a boss or removing it as a cut, in one or both directions.

40 minutesbeginner
→04

Revolve

Model round, rotationally symmetric parts efficiently by revolving a half-profile sketch around an axis instead of drawing the full shape.

35 minutesbeginner
→05

Sweep

Model a constant cross-section that follows a curved path — like a tube, a hose fitting, or a formed bracket — with the sweep feature.

40 minutesintermediate
→06

Loft

Blend between two or more different cross-section profiles smoothly with a loft, for shapes that change form along their length.

40 minutesintermediate
→07

Boolean Operations: Cut, Join, and Intersect

Combine separate solid bodies with join, cut, and intersect — the same logic underlying every add- or remove-material feature.

40 minutesintermediate
→08

Fillets and Chamfers

Round or bevel a sharp edge with fillet and chamfer features, and know why edge-selection order in the tree matters more than it looks like it should.

35 minutesbeginner
→09

Holes and Hole Features

Model manufacturing-accurate holes with the dedicated hole feature — simple, counterbored, countersunk, and tapped — instead of a plain extrude cut.

40 minutesbeginner
→10

Patterns: Rectangular, Circular, and Mirror

Repeat a feature across a grid, around an axis, or across a plane of symmetry with pattern tools, keeping every copy tied to one controlling feature.

40 minutesbeginner
→11

Modeling Threads

Represent threads with a fast cosmetic callout for most modeling work, or true helical geometry when the thread form itself must be visible.

40 minutesintermediate
→12

Assembly Modeling: Mates and Constraints

Position and constrain multiple parts relative to each other in an assembly using mates, and understand how a part's degrees of freedom get removed.

45 minutesintermediate
→13

Assembly Motion and Interference Detection

Drive an assembly's remaining free degrees of freedom to check motion, and run interference detection to catch collisions before parts are ever cut.

40 minutesintermediate
→14

Drawing Generation from 3D Models

Generate a 2D manufacturing drawing directly from a 3D model, keeping views and dimensions linked to the model so a design change updates the print automatically.

45 minutesintermediate
→15

DFM: Internal Corner Radii and Tool Access

Design internal corners a real rotating tool can actually cut, instead of a sharp corner that only exists in the CAD model.

40 minutesintermediate
→16

DFM: Wall Thickness and Thin Feature Considerations

Design walls and thin features that survive both the cutting forces of machining and the clamping forces of workholding, not just the finished part's own service loads.

40 minutesintermediate
→17

DFM: Workholding Access and Minimizing Setups

Design a part so it can actually be clamped and reached with a tool, and so features cluster onto as few setups as possible.

40 minutesintermediate
→18

DFM: Tolerance Stack-Up Awareness

Model and dimension a design so tolerances don't compound invisibly across a chain of features or an assembly of parts.

45 minutesadvanced
