Reference

Shop math formulas

The calculations that come up at the machine, in metric, each with the variables named and a worked example. Cutting speeds are m/min, diameters and feeds are mm.

Speeds and feedsMaterial removal and powerTurningDrilling and tappingGeometry and setup

Speeds and feeds

The four numbers you set before every cut. Everything else follows from these.

Spindle speed from cutting speed

n = (vc × 1000) / (π × D)rev/min

n
Spindle speedrev/min
vc
Cutting speed, from the tool cataloguem/min
D
Cutter diameter, or workpiece diameter when turningmm

Worked example

12 mm carbide end mill in mild steel, vc = 120 m/min

n = (120 × 1000) / (π × 12) = 120000 / 37.70

n ≈ 3183 rev/min

Cutting speed from spindle speed

vc = (π × D × n) / 1000m/min

vc
Cutting speedm/min
D
Cutter or workpiece diametermm
n
Spindle speedrev/min

Worked example

50 mm face mill running at 2000 rev/min

vc = (π × 50 × 2000) / 1000 = 314159 / 1000

vc ≈ 314 m/min

Table feed rate

vf = fz × z × nmm/min

vf
Feed rate, the F word in your G-codemm/min
fz
Feed per tooth, from the tool cataloguemm/tooth
z
Number of flutes
n
Spindle speedrev/min

Worked example

4-flute cutter, fz = 0.05 mm/tooth, n = 3183 rev/min

vf = 0.05 × 4 × 3183

vf ≈ 637 mm/min → F637

Feed per tooth from feed rate

fz = vf / (z × n)mm/tooth

fz
Feed per toothmm/tooth
vf
Feed ratemm/min
z
Number of flutes
n
Spindle speedrev/min

Use this to check a program someone else wrote. If fz lands far below the catalogue value the tool is rubbing, not cutting, and it will work-harden the material and fail early.

Feed per revolution

f = vf / nmm/rev

f
Feed per revolution, the turning and drilling feedmm/rev
vf
Feed ratemm/min
n
Spindle speedrev/min

Lathes and drills are programmed in mm/rev under G95. Mills are programmed in mm/min under G94.

Material removal and power

What the cut demands from the machine. Check these before committing to a heavy roughing pass.

Material removal rate — milling

Q = (ap × ae × vf) / 1000cm³/min

Q
Material removal ratecm³/min
ap
Axial depth of cutmm
ae
Radial width of cutmm
vf
Feed ratemm/min

Worked example

ap = 10 mm, ae = 6 mm, vf = 637 mm/min

Q = (10 × 6 × 637) / 1000 = 38220 / 1000

Q ≈ 38.2 cm³/min

Material removal rate — turning

Q = vc × ap × fcm³/min

Q
Material removal ratecm³/min
vc
Cutting speedm/min
ap
Depth of cutmm
f
Feed per revolutionmm/rev

Net cutting power

Pc = (Q × kc) / (60 × 1000 × η)kW

Pc
Power at the spindlekW
Q
Material removal ratecm³/min
kc
Specific cutting force for the materialN/mm²
η
Machine efficiency, typically 0.75 to 0.9

Rough kc values: aluminium 700, mild steel 1700, stainless 2100, titanium 1900 N/mm². If Pc approaches your spindle rating, reduce ae before reducing vc.

Turning

Lathe-specific work, where the diameter changes under the tool.

Surface roughness from feed

Ra ≈ (f² × 1000) / (8 × rε)µm

Ra
Theoretical arithmetic mean roughnessµm
f
Feed per revolutionmm/rev
rε
Insert corner radiusmm

Worked example

f = 0.15 mm/rev, rε = 0.8 mm

Ra = (0.15² × 1000) / (8 × 0.8) = 22.5 / 6.4

Ra ≈ 3.5 µm

This is the theoretical floor. Real Ra is worse. To halve roughness, halve the feed or double the corner radius — but a larger radius pushes harder and can chatter on slender parts.

Cutting time

tc = L / (f × n)min

tc
Time for the passmin
L
Length of cutmm
f
Feed per revolutionmm/rev
n
Spindle speedrev/min

Taper per side

α = atan((D − d) / (2L))degrees

α
Included half-angle, what you set the compound todegrees
D
Large diametermm
d
Small diametermm
L
Length between the two diametersmm

Worked example

D = 40 mm, d = 30 mm, L = 60 mm

α = atan((40 − 30) / 120) = atan(0.0833)

α ≈ 4.76°

Drilling and tapping

Hole work, where getting the drill size wrong is the difference between a thread and a scrapped part.

Tap drill size — metric coarse

d = D − Pmm

d
Tap drill diametermm
D
Nominal thread diametermm
P
Thread pitchmm

Worked example

M10 × 1.5 coarse thread

d = 10 − 1.5

d = 8.5 mm drill

This gives roughly 75% thread engagement, which is the standard target. Chasing 100% engagement gains almost no strength and breaks taps.

Thread engagement from drill size

E = ((D − d) / (1.0825 × P)) × 100%

E
Percentage of full thread%
D
Nominal thread diametermm
d
Actual drill diametermm
P
Thread pitchmm

Below about 60% the thread is too weak to trust. Above about 80% tapping torque climbs steeply for very little extra strength.

Drill point depth allowance

h ≈ 0.3 × Dmm

h
Extra depth to clear the pointmm
D
Drill diametermm

For a standard 118° point. Add h to the required full-diameter depth so the flat portion of the hole reaches the drawing dimension. A 140° point needs about 0.18 × D.

Geometry and setup

The trigonometry that comes up at the machine, usually while something is already clamped.

Bolt circle coordinates

X = R × cos(θ) Y = R × sin(θ)mm

X, Y
Hole position from the circle centremm
R
Bolt circle radiusmm
θ
Angle of the hole, measured from +Xdegrees

Worked example

6 holes on a 100 mm diameter circle, first hole at 0°

θ step = 360 / 6 = 60°, R = 50 mm. Hole 2: X = 50 × cos(60°), Y = 50 × sin(60°)

Hole 2 at X25.0 Y43.301

Sine bar stack height

h = L × sin(α)mm

h
Gauge block stack heightmm
L
Sine bar centre distance, usually 100 or 200 mmmm
α
Required angledegrees

Worked example

100 mm sine bar set to 15°

h = 100 × sin(15°) = 100 × 0.2588

h = 25.882 mm of gauge blocks

Chord length across a circle

c = 2 × R × sin(θ / 2)mm

c
Straight-line distance between two points on the circlemm
R
Circle radiusmm
θ
Angle subtended at the centredegrees

Useful for checking hole-to-hole spacing on a bolt circle with calipers before you trust the program.
