Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Learn what PPE is required in a machine shop, how to select the right protection, and common PPE mistakes that lead to injuries.

Learning objectives

  • Identify all required PPE for machine shop work
  • Select appropriate eye, ear, hand, and foot protection
  • Understand when NOT to wear certain items (gloves, loose clothing)
  • Inspect PPE for damage before use

The PPE Mindset

PPE is your last line of defense. It doesn’t eliminate hazards — it protects you when everything else fails. A chip guard should catch 99% of flying chips, but safety glasses catch the one that gets through.

Rule #1: PPE is non-negotiable. It doesn’t matter if you’re “just checking something real quick” or “only running one part.” Full PPE, every time.

Required PPE in a Machine Shop

Eye Protection — Always On

Safety glasses with side shields are worn at all times in the shop area — not just when operating machines. Chips can fly from someone else’s machine. Coolant can splash unexpectedly.

Requirements:

  • Must meet ANSI Z87.1 standard (look for the “Z87” marking on the lens or frame)
  • Side shields are mandatory — regular prescription glasses are NOT safety glasses
  • Must be clean and unscratched — scratched lenses reduce visibility and weaken the lens

When to upgrade:

  • Face shield — for grinding, heavy milling with poor chip control, or high-pressure coolant
  • Goggles — when working with chemicals, coolant concentrate, or solvents

Hearing Protection — When Needed

CNC machines can easily exceed 85 dB during aggressive cuts, especially in aluminum or with older machines.

Options:

TypeNRR RatingBest For
Foam earplugs25–33 dBHighest protection, daily use
Reusable plugs20–27 dBConvenience, moderate noise
Over-ear muffs22–31 dBEasy on/off, intermittent exposure

Tip: NRR (Noise Reduction Rating) is measured in a lab. Real-world protection is typically NRR minus 7 dB, divided by 2. So an NRR 29 earplug provides roughly (29-7)/2 = 11 dB of actual reduction.

Foot Protection — Steel Toes

Steel-toed safety boots are required in most shops. Workpieces, tooling, and fixtures are heavy — dropping a 50 lb vise on your foot without steel toes will break bones.

Requirements:

  • ASTM F2413-rated
  • Steel or composite safety toe
  • Slip-resistant sole (shop floors get slippery with coolant)
  • No open-toed shoes, ever

Hand Protection — It Depends

This is where many beginners make a dangerous mistake:

⚠️ NEVER wear gloves while operating a lathe, mill, or drill press with rotating parts. Gloves can catch on spinning workpieces or tools and pull your hand in. This causes severe, life-changing injuries.

When to wear gloves:

  • Handling sharp-edged parts (deburring, loading/unloading)
  • Working with chemicals (nitrile gloves)
  • Moving heavy stock material

When NOT to wear gloves:

  • Operating any machine with rotating spindles
  • Near any rotating tooling or workpiece
  • Near chuck jaws, collets, or live centers

Body Protection

  • No loose clothing — tuck in shirts, roll up sleeves, no ties or scarves
  • No jewelry — rings, necklaces, bracelets, watches can all get caught
  • Long hair must be tied back and contained (in a cap if necessary)
  • Shop apron or close-fitting work clothes recommended

PPE Inspection Checklist

Before each shift, check your PPE:

  • Safety glasses: clean, no scratches, side shields intact, Z87 rated
  • Hearing protection: clean, foam plugs not worn out, muffs seal properly
  • Safety boots: intact steel toe, good tread, laces secured
  • Clothing: close-fitting, no loose ends, sleeves rolled up
  • No jewelry, watches, or dangling items

Common PPE Mistakes

  1. “I’ll just be a second” — removing safety glasses for “quick” tasks. Most eye injuries happen during “quick” tasks.
  2. Wearing gloves at the lathe — this is one of the most dangerous mistakes in a machine shop.
  3. Scratched safety glasses — they reduce visibility, causing you to lean closer to the machine.
  4. Wrong hearing protection — cotton balls do NOT count as hearing protection.
  5. Loose clothing at a lathe — the spinning chuck will grab fabric instantly.

Self-Check Questions

1. When should you NOT wear gloves in a machine shop?

Show Answer

Never wear gloves while operating any machine with rotating parts (lathe, mill, drill press). Gloves can catch on spinning workpieces, chucks, or tooling and pull your hand into the machine.

2. What does the ANSI Z87.1 marking on safety glasses mean?

Show Answer

It means the glasses meet the American National Standards Institute’s impact resistance requirements for safety eyewear. Always look for the “Z87” marking on the lens or frame — without it, they’re not safety rated.

3. Why is jewelry banned in machine shops?

Show Answer

Rings, necklaces, bracelets, and watches can catch on rotating parts, sharp edges, or moving workpieces. A ring caught in a rotating chuck can deglove a finger — one of the most severe shop injuries.
