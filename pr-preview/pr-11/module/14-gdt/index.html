Geometric Dimensioning & Tolerancing (GD&T)

ASME Y14.5 standard — datum systems, form/orientation/location/runout tolerances, MMC/LMC, and interpreting GD&T on part prints.

Lessons

01

Why GD&T: Function-Based Dimensioning vs. Coordinate Tolerancing

Understand why GD&T exists — controlling a feature's actual function instead of just its X/Y coordinates — and the ambiguity problem it solves.

50 minutesintermediate
→02

Feature Control Frames: Reading and Interpreting

Read a GD&T feature control frame left to right — geometric characteristic, tolerance value and zone shape, material modifier, and datum references in precedence order.

45 minutesintermediate
→03

Datum Selection: Primary, Secondary, Tertiary — and Their Order

Choose and order datum features so the datum reference frame reflects how a part actually functions and gets held, connecting directly to the 3-2-1 locating principle.

45 minutesintermediate
→04

Material Condition Modifiers: MMC and LMC

Understand MMC and LMC as the worst-case extremes of a feature's size range, and what applying one to a tolerance actually changes.

45 minutesadvanced
→05

RFS and Choosing the Right Modifier

Understand RFS as GD&T's strict default — no bonus tolerance regardless of produced size — and know when to specify it versus MMC or LMC.

40 minutesadvanced
→06

Bonus Tolerance

Calculate how much extra positional tolerance a feature actually earns as it departs from MMC, and why that extra tolerance is functionally real, not a loophole.

45 minutesadvanced
→07

Form Controls: Flatness and Straightness

Control a single surface's own form with flatness and straightness — the two GD&T controls that need no datum reference at all.

40 minutesintermediate
→08

Form Controls: Circularity and Cylindricity

Control a round feature's cross-sectional roundness with circularity, or its entire surface at once with the more comprehensive cylindricity.

40 minutesintermediate
→09

Orientation Controls: Perpendicularity, Angularity, and Parallelism

Control a feature's angle relative to a datum with perpendicularity, angularity, and parallelism — the same underlying control at three different target angles.

40 minutesintermediate
→10

Location: Position (True Position)

Locate a feature's actual center within a cylindrical tolerance zone around its theoretically exact true position — GD&T's most-used control.

45 minutesadvanced
→11

Location: Concentricity and Symmetry

Understand concentricity and symmetry's median-point tolerance zones, and why modern practice usually replaces them with position or runout.

40 minutesadvanced
→12

Runout: Circular Runout and Total Runout

Check a rotating part's surface deviation directly with a dial indicator — circular runout element by element, or total runout across the entire surface at once.

40 minutesintermediate
→13

Profile: Profile of a Line and Profile of a Surface

Control a complex 2D outline or a full 3D surface within a tolerance band — GD&T's most versatile control, capable of governing size, form, orientation, and location at once.

45 minutesadvanced
→14

How GD&T Callouts Affect Machining Strategy

Read a GD&T callout for what it actually demands from the machining process — setup order, datum-referenced operations, and where bonus tolerance can ease a tight job.

45 minutesadvanced
→15

Fixture Design for Datum Reference

Build a fixture that physically simulates the print's datum reference frame, so the part is held exactly the way its GD&T callouts assume it's measured.

45 minutesadvanced
→16

CMM Inspection of GD&T Features

Verify a GD&T callout with a CMM by establishing the same datum reference frame the print defines, then evaluating the feature's actual measured geometry against its tolerance zone.

40 minutesadvanced
→17

Functional Gauging vs. CMM Inspection

Check an MMC-toleranced feature in seconds with a fixed functional gauge built to the worst-case virtual condition, and know when that tradeoff beats CMM flexibility.

40 minutesadvanced
→18

Reading a Fully-Toleranced Print: Putting It All Together

Walk a real, fully GD&T-toleranced print in the right order — datums first, then feature control frames, then each callout's real functional and manufacturing consequence.

50 minutesadvanced
