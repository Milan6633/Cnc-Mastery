Heat-Resistant Super Alloys and Titanium (ISO S)

Classify heat resistant super alloys and titanium in the ISO S group and describe their machining characteristics.

Learning objectives

  • List the base elements of heat resistant super alloys.
  • Identify the groups and conditions of ISO S materials.
  • Explain the effect of cobalt content on properties.
  • State the specific cutting force ranges for HRSA and titanium.

What Are Heat Resistant Super Alloys?

ISO group S covers the most demanding materials in metal cutting.

Key concept

Under the HRSA label sits a wide range of heavily alloyed materials built on an iron, nickel, cobalt, or titanium base.

These materials are found in the aerospace, gas turbine, and power generator industries, where components must survive high temperatures and high loads.

Groups and Conditions

ISO S materials are classified by their base element and by their condition.

GroupBase element
Fe-basedIron
Ni-basedNickel
Co-basedCobalt
Ti-basedTitanium
Mo-based / W-basedMolybdenum / Tungsten

Conditions include: annealed, solution heat treated, aged, rolled, forged, and cast.

The Effect of Cobalt

Alloy content changes the properties of the material, and cobalt is the key driver in HRSA.

Key concept

Push the alloy content higher and tilt it toward cobalt over nickel, and the material rewards you with better heat resistance, more tensile strength, and stronger resistance to corrosion.

Machining Characteristics

  • HRSA and titanium are long-chipping materials.
  • Chip control is difficult, with segmented chips.
  • Cutting forces and power required are quite high.
Safety warning

The rake angle you need flips with the tool material — go negative when running ceramics, positive when running carbide.

Key concept

The specific cutting force is 2400–3100 N/mm² for HRSA and 1300–1400 N/mm² for titanium.

Self-Check

1. Which base elements make up heat resistant super alloys?

Show Answer

High-alloyed iron, nickel, cobalt, or titanium based materials. Molybdenum and tungsten based alloys are also found in the group.

2. What happens when a HRSA has more cobalt than nickel?

Show Answer

It gains better resistance against heat, increased tensile strength, and higher corrosive resistance.

3. What is the kc1 range for titanium?

Show Answer

1300–1400 N/mm².

4. Which rake angle is used with carbide versus ceramics?

Show Answer

A positive rake angle with carbide, and a negative rake angle with ceramics.
