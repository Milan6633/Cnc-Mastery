Steel (ISO P)

Classify steels in the ISO P group and describe their machining characteristics.

Learning objectives

  • Define steel by composition and production process.
  • Distinguish unalloyed, low-alloyed, high-alloyed, and sintered steels.
  • State the specific cutting force range for ISO P materials.
  • Describe chip control for low- and high-carbon steel.

What Is Steel?

Steel is the largest group in the metal cutting area, which is why it carries its own ISO letter, P.

Key concept

At its core, steel is simply an iron-based alloy (Fe as the dominant element), made by melting the raw components together.

You will meet it either left soft, or hardened and tempered up to about 400 HB, and carbon content is what sorts it into categories:

  • Below 0.8% carbon with nothing else added, the steel is classed as unalloyed — iron and carbon alone.
  • Add elements like Ni, Cr, Mo, V, or W and stay under 1.7% carbon, and the steel is classed as alloyed.

Subgroups Within ISO P

ISO P is further divided by the amount and type of alloying elements:

SubgroupDefinition
Unalloyed steelCarbon below 0.8%, iron only
Low-alloyed steelUp to 5% alloying elements (≤5%)
High-alloyed steelMore than 5% alloying elements (>5%)
Sintered steelPowder-based, pressed and sintered

Machining Characteristics

ISO P materials are generally the most forgiving group to machine, but carbon content still changes behavior.

  • Steel is a long-chipping material.
  • Chip control is relatively easy and smooth.
  • Low-carbon steel is tough and sticky, and needs sharp cutting edges.
  • High-carbon steel is more brittle.
Key concept

ISO P steel carries a kc1 of 1500–3100 N/mm². Move within that band and both the cutting force and the power draw stay fairly predictable from one steel job to the next.

Pro tip

Carbon content drives the biggest practical difference. A sticky low-carbon steel demands a sharp edge, while a brittle high-carbon steel chips and breaks more readily.

ISO P Application Areas

The ISO P nomenclature uses numbered application areas from P01 to P50. These range from internal and external finishing turning at high cutting speed with small chip areas (P01), through general turning and milling, to P50, where very high toughness in the tool is needed for extremely tough conditions at low cutting speed and large chip areas.

Self-Check

1. What is the carbon limit for an unalloyed steel?

Show Answer

Below 0.8% carbon, with iron as the only component and no other alloying elements.

2. How do low-alloyed and high-alloyed steels differ?

Show Answer

Low-alloyed steels contain up to 5% alloying elements (≤5%), while high-alloyed steels contain more than 5% (>5%).

3. What is the kc1 range for ISO P steel?

Show Answer

1500–3100 N/mm².

4. Why does low-carbon steel need a sharp cutting edge?

Show Answer

Low-carbon steel is tough and sticky, so a sharp edge is needed to cut cleanly and control the chip.
