## A Groove That Self-Centers Round Stock

The workholding module covered vises, soft jaws, strap clamps, fixture plates, and vacuum and magnetic chucks in depth — the locate-support-clamp sequence and clamping-force principles covered there apply just as much on the milling table as anywhere else. A **V-block** is the one common mill workholding fixture not yet covered, and it solves a specific problem: holding **round stock** without needing to indicate it true every single time.

A V-block is a precision-ground block with a V-shaped groove, typically at 90°, cut into its top. Round stock laid into that groove naturally settles so its axis lies along the V's own centerline — because the two angled faces of the V contact the round stock symmetrically, any reasonably round bar of a size that fits the groove centers itself automatically, without needing an indicator sweep to confirm it.

:::key-concept
A V-block's self-centering comes from geometry alone, not measurement. Two symmetric angled faces contacting a round part guarantee the part's axis sits on the V's centerline — this is the same self-centering logic behind a 3-jaw chuck's scroll mechanism, applied to a stationary fixture instead of a rotating one.
:::

## Matched Pairs for Longer Stock

V-blocks are commonly used in **matched pairs**, supporting a longer round part at two points along its length — both blocks cut to the same groove angle and height, so a shaft resting in both settles onto the same true axis at both ends. This is particularly useful for operations that need the part's axis to stay consistent and known across its full length, without the setup complexity of chucking it or building a dedicated fixture.

## Cross-Drilling: The Natural V-Block Job

Drilling a hole perpendicular to a round part's own axis — cross-drilling a shaft or pin — is one of the most common V-block applications on a manual mill. Because the V-block centers the stock automatically, the spindle only needs to be positioned correctly along the part's length; the block itself guarantees the part is centered under the spindle in the other direction, with no separate indicating step required for that axis.

A strap clamp or a U-shaped clamp typically holds the round stock down into the V-block from above, spanning over the part and anchoring into the block or the table — the same T-slot clamping hardware covered in the workholding module, adapted to the V-block's specific geometry.

:::pro-tip
When cross-drilling several identical round parts, a V-block setup lets each new part drop into the same self-centering position with essentially no re-indicating between parts — a significant time saving over indicating each part individually in a 4-jaw-style setup.
:::

## Magnetic V-Blocks

A **magnetic V-block** builds a magnetic base directly into the fixture, holding ferrous round stock in the groove without a separate strap clamp at all — useful on a surface plate for layout work or on the mill table for lighter-duty operations where the magnetic hold provides adequate security. As with any magnetic workholding, this only works on ferromagnetic material, and the same holding-force limits covered for magnetic chucks in the workholding module apply here too.

## Self-Check Questions

**1. Why does round stock automatically center itself when placed in a V-block's groove?**

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

The two angled faces of the V contact the round stock symmetrically, so any round part that fits the groove settles with its axis on the V's own centerline — a geometric result, not something that needs to be measured or indicated.
</details>

**2. What advantage does a matched V-block pair give when supporting a longer shaft?**

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

Both blocks, cut to the same groove angle and height, put the shaft on the same true axis at both support points, keeping the part's axis consistent along its full length.
</details>

**3. Why is cross-drilling round stock a particularly natural application for a V-block?**

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

The V-block automatically centers the part under the spindle in one direction, so only position along the part's length needs to be set — no separate indicating step is needed for centering, unlike round stock held in a 4-jaw chuck.
</details>

**4. What holds round stock down into a V-block during cutting?**

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

Typically a strap clamp or U-shaped clamp spanning over the part and anchoring into the block or table, using the same T-slot clamping hardware covered for general mill workholding.
</details>

**5. What limitation does a magnetic V-block share with a magnetic chuck?**

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

It only works on ferromagnetic material, and its holding force is subject to the same limits covered for magnetic chucks — not suited to heavy, interrupted cutting loads.
</details>
