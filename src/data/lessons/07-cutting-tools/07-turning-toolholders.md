## The Holder Sets the Entering Angle

The tool holder, not the insert alone, determines the entering angle: its tip seat combines with the insert shape to set KAPR. Secure insert and holder clamping is essential for stability, so the holder choice starts from the operation.

:::key-concept
Tool holder types are defined by the entering (lead) angle, the insert shape and the insert size.
:::

## Clamping Systems

- Rigid clamping: negative inserts, excellent clamping, easy indexing.
- Lever clamping: negative inserts, free chip flow, easy indexing.
- Screw clamping: positive inserts, secure clamping, free chip flow.
- Polygon/pin screw clamping: positive inserts, a mating polygon or pin interface adds extra location accuracy on top of the screw's clamping force, for very secure and highly repeatable seating.

:::pro-tip
For negative inserts use rigid or lever clamping; for positive inserts use screw clamping. Match the clamping to the insert style.
:::

## External Turning: Four Application Areas

| Operation | Entering angles | Typical inserts |
|---|---|---|
| Longitudinal turning | 95°, 93° | C 80°, also D 55°, W 80°, T 60° |
| Facing | 75°, 95°/91° | C 80°, S 90° |
| Profiling | 93° (in-copying 22–27°) | D 55°, V 35° |
| Pocketing | Neutral 90° | Round |

### Longitudinal Turning

The most common operation. The C-style 80° insert in a 95° or 93° holder is the standard choice; D, W and T shapes are alternatives.

### Facing

Feed toward centre. Cutting speed falls progressively as the tool approaches the axis, so watch the effective speed. Entering angles of 75° and 95°/91° are common.

### Profiling

Versatility and accessibility decide. A 93° entering angle (lead −3°) allows an in-copying angle of 22–27°. Keep a free cutting angle of at least 2° between workpiece and insert.

:::warning
Always maintain at least a 2° free cutting angle in profiling, or the flank will rub and the edge will fail.
:::

### Pocketing

Round inserts in a neutral 90° holder suit plunge turning because they can feed both radially and axially.

## Internal Turning

Internal turning is restricted by the hole diameter and length. Push the bar diameter toward the largest size the hole allows, and pull the overhang back to the shortest length the job permits — both work in your favor against deflection, and neither can be maximized without limit.

:::key-concept
More boring setups are ruined by trapped chips than by almost any other single factor, so getting them out of the hole deserves as much attention as the cut itself. Coolant fed through the bar helps flush them clear, and running the operation with the tool inverted keeps chips falling away from the edge instead of recutting through them.
:::

### Entering Angle

Select an entering angle close to 90° (lead close to 0°) to push the cutting force axially along the bar.

:::warning
75° is the floor for entering angle in boring work. Drop below it — past 15° of lead — and the force pushing radially against the bar climbs sharply, bending the bar off its line.
:::

### Insert Choice

Because the bar is the flexible link in the chain, keeping cutting force down matters more here than in external turning — which is why single-sided inserts with 7° clearance are the default starting point for boring from 6 mm diameter upward. Their lower force translates directly into less bar deflection. Save negative inserts for cases where the setup is genuinely rigid and the overhang is short.

### Internal Application Areas

- Longitudinal turning: C 80° insert, 95° or 93° bars.
- Facing: entering angle close to 90°.
- Profiling: 93° bars, D 55° and V 35° inserts.
- Back boring: reverse feed for shoulders under 90°, 93° bars with D 55° inserts.

## Overhang and Clamping Rules

The boring bar is the weak link, so overhang is tightly controlled by bar type.

| Bar type | Maximum overhang |
|---|---|
| Solid steel | 4 × DMM |
| Carbide bar | 6 × DMM |
| Short dampened bar | 7 × DMM |
| Long dampened bar | 10 × DMM |
| Carbide-reinforced dampened bar | 14 × DMM |

:::key-concept
DMM is the bar diameter. A carbide-reinforced dampened bar can reach 14 × DMM, versus only 4 × DMM for a solid steel bar.
:::

Clamping length should be 3 to 4 times the bar diameter to balance cutting forces. Dampened bars are available from 10 mm diameter. A quick-locating clamping sleeve with a spring-loaded plunger seats into a groove on the bar, so centre height comes out correct every time without manual shimming.
