## Maintenance Before a Problem Appears

**Scheduled (preventive) maintenance** performs specific maintenance tasks at defined intervals — calendar time or machine usage hours — regardless of whether any problem has actually appeared yet. This is the direct opposite of waiting for something to visibly go wrong before addressing it, and it's the foundation most machine reliability programs are built on.

## Lubrication

A CNC machine's ways, ballscrews, spindle bearings, and gearboxes all depend on a properly maintained lubricant film to prevent direct metal-to-metal contact between moving precision surfaces. Missing a scheduled lubrication interval, or using the wrong lubricant type for a given component, accelerates wear dramatically — and because ways and ballscrews are exactly the precision surfaces that define the machine's actual positioning accuracy, that accelerated wear translates directly into degraded machine performance over time.

:::key-concept
Way and ballscrew lubrication isn't a minor housekeeping task — it's directly protecting the same precision surfaces that determine how accurately the machine can actually position itself. Neglected lubrication doesn't just risk a mechanical failure eventually; it degrades the machine's fundamental positioning accuracy well before any catastrophic failure occurs.
:::

## Filter Changes

Hydraulic filters, coolant filters, and way lubrication filters all need periodic replacement. A clogged filter creates two distinct problems depending on the system's design: it can **restrict flow**, starving a component of the lubrication or coolant it actually needs, or — if the system includes a bypass valve for when a filter clogs — it can let **unfiltered, contaminated fluid** through, defeating the filter's entire purpose at exactly the moment it's most needed.

## Way Wipers

**Way wipers** are seals that ride along the machine's precision ways and slides as the axes move, specifically to keep chips, coolant, and debris off those surfaces. A torn, missing, or degraded way wiper lets abrasive chips and debris reach the ways directly — the same kind of physical way damage covered in the manual machining module's lathe bed care lesson, just introduced continuously through normal machine operation rather than from a single dropped object.

:::warning
A damaged way wiper doesn't cause an immediate, obvious failure — it introduces a slow, continuous source of abrasive contamination onto precision surfaces that accumulates gradually, the same way skipped lubrication does. By the time the resulting wear shows up as a noticeable accuracy problem, real damage has often already occurred. Inspecting way wipers as part of routine scheduled maintenance catches this before it reaches that point.
:::
