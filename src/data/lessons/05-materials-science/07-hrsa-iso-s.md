## What Are Heat Resistant Super Alloys?

ISO group S covers the most demanding materials in metal cutting.

:::key-concept
Under the HRSA label sits a wide range of heavily alloyed materials built on
an iron, nickel, cobalt, or titanium base.
:::

These materials are found in the aerospace, gas turbine, and power generator
industries, where components must survive high temperatures and high loads.

## Groups and Conditions

ISO S materials are classified by their base element and by their condition.

| Group | Base element |
|-------|--------------|
| Fe-based | Iron |
| Ni-based | Nickel |
| Co-based | Cobalt |
| Ti-based | Titanium |
| Mo-based / W-based | Molybdenum / Tungsten |

Conditions include: annealed, solution heat treated, aged, rolled, forged, and
cast.

## The Effect of Cobalt

Alloy content changes the properties of the material, and cobalt is the key
driver in HRSA.

:::key-concept
Push the alloy content higher and tilt it toward cobalt over nickel, and the
material rewards you with better heat resistance, more tensile strength, and
stronger resistance to corrosion.
:::

## Machining Characteristics

- HRSA and titanium are long-chipping materials.
- Chip control is difficult, with segmented chips.
- Cutting forces and power required are quite high.

:::warning
The rake angle you need flips with the tool material — go negative when
running ceramics, positive when running carbide.
:::

:::key-concept
The specific cutting force is 2400–3100 N/mm² for HRSA and 1300–1400 N/mm² for
titanium.
:::

## Self-Check

**1. Which base elements make up heat resistant super alloys?**

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

High-alloyed iron, nickel, cobalt, or titanium based materials. Molybdenum and
tungsten based alloys are also found in the group.
</details>

**2. What happens when a HRSA has more cobalt than nickel?**

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

It gains better resistance against heat, increased tensile strength, and
higher corrosive resistance.
</details>

**3. What is the kc1 range for titanium?**

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

1300–1400 N/mm².
</details>

**4. Which rake angle is used with carbide versus ceramics?**

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

A positive rake angle with carbide, and a negative rake angle with ceramics.
</details>
