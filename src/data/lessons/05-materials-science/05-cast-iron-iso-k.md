## What Is Cast Iron?

Cast iron belongs to ISO group K and cuts very differently from steel despite
both being iron-based.

:::key-concept
Cast iron is an iron–carbon (Fe-C) composition with a relatively high silicon
content of 1–3%. Its carbon content is over 2%, which is the maximum solubility
of carbon in the austenitic phase.
:::

That high carbon content is the reason cast iron chips short instead of
forming long ribbons.

## Forms of Cast Iron

There are three main forms, plus two more you will meet in practice:

| Form | Abbreviation |
|------|--------------|
| Gray cast iron | GCI |
| Nodular / SG iron | NCI / SG |
| Compacted graphite iron | CGI |
| Malleable cast iron | — |
| Austempered ductile iron | ADI |

## The Effect of Alloying Elements

Cast irons are often alloyed to raise strength, but there is always a trade-off.

:::warning
Adding chromium, molybdenum, or vanadium buys strength and hardness through
the carbides they form, but that trade comes at the cost of machinability.
:::

## Machining Characteristics

- Cast iron is a short-chipping material.
- Chip control is good in all conditions.
- Cutting forces are moderate.
- Machining at higher speeds creates abrasive wear.

:::key-concept
The specific cutting force kc1 for ISO K cast iron is 790–1350 N/mm², the
lowest of the ferrous groups.
:::

## Gray vs. Ductile Iron

Do not treat all cast iron the same. The chip tells you which one you are
cutting.

:::key-concept
Gray and ductile iron are not interchangeable at the chip pan. Gray iron
tends to disintegrate into a fine, powdery chip, whereas ductile iron holds
together and breaks the way a plain steel would.
:::

## Self-Check

**1. What carbon and silicon content defines cast iron?**

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

Carbon content over 2% and silicon content of 1–3%.
</details>

**2. What are the three main forms of cast iron?**

<details>

Gray (GCI), nodular (NCI), and compacted graphite (CGI). Malleable cast iron
and austempered ductile iron (ADI) are also common.
</details>

**3. How do Cr, Mo, and V affect cast iron?**

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

They form carbides that increase strength and hardness but lower machinability.
</details>

**4. How do chips differ between gray and ductile iron?**

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

Gray iron tends to break down into fine, powdery chips, while ductile iron
breaks more the way plain steel does.
</details>
