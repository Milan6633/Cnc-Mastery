## What Is Hardened Steel?

Of the six ISO groups, H covers the narrowest slice of machining work.

:::key-concept
What lands in ISO H has been hardened and tempered up into the 45–65 HRC
range.
:::

In practice, though, the parts you actually see going through hard turning
usually sit higher still, in the 55–68 HRC band.

The group also includes other very hard materials:

- Chilled cast iron.
- Ferro-TiC.
- Stellites.

## Machining Characteristics

Hardened steel is machined in a completely different regime from soft steel.

- It is a long-chipping material.
- Chip control is fair.
- Cutting forces and power required are quite high.

:::warning
A negative rake angle is required. Machining hardened steel is heat-generating
and abrasive, which places heavy demands on the cutting edge.
:::

:::key-concept
The specific cutting force kc1 for ISO H hardened steel is 2550–4870 N/mm², the
highest of all six ISO groups.
:::

## Hard Part Turning

Hardened steel is almost always finished rather than roughed, and it demands a
specific class of cutting tool material.

:::pro-tip
Hardened steel is typically finish-turned (hard part turning) with CBN or
ceramic grades, which retain hardness and wear resistance at the high
temperatures and forces involved.
:::
