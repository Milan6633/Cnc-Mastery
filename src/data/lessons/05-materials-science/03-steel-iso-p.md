## What Is Steel?

Steel is the largest group in the metal cutting area, which is why it carries
its own ISO letter, P.

:::key-concept
At its core, steel is simply an iron-based alloy (Fe as the dominant
element), made by melting the raw components together.
:::

You will meet it either left soft, or hardened and tempered up to about
400 HB, and carbon content is what sorts it into categories:

- Below 0.8% carbon with nothing else added, the steel is classed as
  unalloyed — iron and carbon alone.
- Add elements like Ni, Cr, Mo, V, or W and stay under 1.7% carbon, and the
  steel is classed as alloyed.

## Subgroups Within ISO P

ISO P is further divided by the amount and type of alloying elements:

| Subgroup | Definition |
|----------|------------|
| Unalloyed steel | Carbon below 0.8%, iron only |
| Low-alloyed steel | Up to 5% alloying elements (≤5%) |
| High-alloyed steel | More than 5% alloying elements (>5%) |
| Sintered steel | Powder-based, pressed and sintered |

## Machining Characteristics

ISO P materials are generally the most forgiving group to machine, but carbon
content still changes behavior.

- Steel is a long-chipping material.
- Chip control is relatively easy and smooth.
- Low-carbon steel is tough and sticky, and needs sharp cutting edges.
- High-carbon steel is more brittle.

:::key-concept
ISO P steel carries a kc1 of 1500–3100 N/mm². Move within that band and both
the cutting force and the power draw stay fairly predictable from one steel
job to the next.
:::

:::pro-tip
Carbon content drives the biggest practical difference. A sticky low-carbon
steel demands a sharp edge, while a brittle high-carbon steel chips and breaks
more readily.
:::

## ISO P Application Areas

The ISO P nomenclature uses numbered application areas from P01 to P50. These
range from internal and external finishing turning at high cutting speed with
small chip areas (P01), through general turning and milling, to P50, where very
high toughness in the tool is needed for extremely tough conditions at low
cutting speed and large chip areas.
