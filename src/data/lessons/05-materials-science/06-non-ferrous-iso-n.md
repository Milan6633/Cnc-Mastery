## What Are Non-Ferrous Materials?

ISO group N covers metals and non-metals that contain no iron.

:::key-concept
Softness is the defining trait here: everything in ISO N is iron-free and
stays below roughly 130 HB.
:::

The group includes:

- Non-ferrous (Al) alloys with up to 22% silicon, which make up the largest part.
- Copper, bronze, and brass.
- Magnesium-based alloys.
- Zinc-based alloys.
- Plastics.
- Composites such as Kevlar.

## Machining Characteristics

- Non-ferrous materials are long-chipping.
- Chip control is relatively easy when the material is alloyed.
- Aluminum is sticky and needs sharp cutting edges.
- ISO N holds to a narrow band for both cutting force and the power a job
  needs.

:::key-concept
The specific cutting force kc1 for ISO N is 350–700 N/mm², the lowest of all
six ISO groups.
:::

:::warning
Low cutting force is not the same thing as low power draw. Yes, non-ferrous
materials take little power per mm³ — but push a high metal removal rate and
the total can climb fast, so run the maximum-power calculation anyway.
:::

## High Speed, Sharp Edges

Non-ferrous materials reward high cutting speeds, but the soft, sticky behavior
of aluminum places a firm demand on the tool.

:::pro-tip
Aluminum is sticky, so a sharp cutting edge and the correct geometry are
essential to avoid built-up edge and poor surface finish.
:::
